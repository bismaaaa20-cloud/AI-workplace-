# AI Workplace Computer Constitution Lifecycle

Version: 1.0.0 — PROPOSED; pending explicit project-owner approval.

## Purpose

This document defines how the AI Workplace computer constitution is established, used during transitions, stabilized, and amended over time. It is inspired by the idea that a system may have different constitutional periods, but it is an original framework for computer and AI governance. It is not Indonesian law, a national constitution, or a replacement for the AI Workplace hierarchy.

## Position under the hierarchy

This lifecycle is subordinate to **MASTER CHARTER**, which is level 6 of the Life-First Ethical Hierarchy. It does not create a new hierarchy, a competing pyramid, or additional authority levels.

The complete precedence remains:

Applicable law and each AI platform's system, developer, safety, and usage constraints remain mandatory external boundaries. No constitutional period or amendment may override them.

## Five constitutional periods

These are lifecycle states or editions of the same governing charter, not five simultaneous constitutions and not five levels of authority.

1. **Initial Computer Constitution** — the first approved baseline that establishes the computer system's purpose, ownership, roles, permissions, and fundamental operating limits.
2. **Interim Computer Constitution** — an explicitly temporary charter used during migration, restructuring, recovery, or major system transition. It must state its scope, start condition, review date or end condition, and the permanent baseline it is intended to lead back to. It cannot suspend higher-level protections.
3. **Permanent Computer Constitution** — the stable, approved charter used for normal operation after the initial setup or transition. “Permanent” means the current stable baseline, not immutable or above amendment.
4. **Amended Computer Constitution** — the same charter after one or more formally approved amendments. Each amendment must identify the previous version, exact changes, reason, impact, approver, and effective point.
5. **Future Amendment Cycle** — the ongoing process for proposing, reviewing, approving, implementing, and auditing later changes. A future proposal does not become active merely because it is newer, committed, or merged.

The interim period is optional. A system may move from its initial approved constitution directly to a stable constitution if no transition requires an interim charter.

## Mandatory amendment controls

- The human project owner is the final approver for internal constitutional changes, subject to higher-level safeguards and external boundaries.
- Proposal, review, approval, implementation, and audit must be distinguishable and recorded.
- A pull request, commit, automated test, or merge alone does not prove owner approval.
- Do not silently replace historical versions. Preserve prior approved texts and an amendment log.
- Every active constitution must have one unambiguous version and effective status.
- If approval, authority, or precedence is unclear, pause the affected constitutional change and ask the owner.
- Temporary rules must expire or be reviewed; they must not become permanent by accident.
- No period may weaken Humanity, Earth, Safety, or Integrity commitments.

## Relationship to software and computer configuration

The constitution governs principles, authority, and change control. It is not itself an executable security boundary. Actual enforcement must be implemented and tested through access controls, permissions, policy checks, logging, backups, and review processes. Documentation alone cannot guarantee that a computer or AI agent will obey a rule.
